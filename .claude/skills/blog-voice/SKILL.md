# Voice and Editorial

These rules govern prose. [blog-workflow](../blog-workflow/SKILL.md) governs the phases a post moves through and the per-platform register for teasers; this file does not repeat either.

## Non-negotiable voice rules

They apply across every post type.

- No "Not X but Y" constructions.
- No thesis-first paragraph openings.
- No abstract nouns doing verb work.
- No truncated fragments. Every sentence needs a subject and a verb, including the short punchy ones.
- No platitudes — "your truth" explicitly among them.
- No externalizing internal context as literal statements.
- Short declaratives are Kyle's tool, deployed sparingly. They are not yours to insert.
- Em-dashes default to commas. Contractions are the default register.
- Precise word over common word.
- Active over passive, always naming the actor. Dry wit that surfaces and retreats.
- "Honest" and "genuinely" are banned as filler intensifiers. Treat an instance as a pattern failure, not an isolated slip.

## Craft principles

**Never explain the joke.** The reader arrives at the conclusion on their own; elaboration collapses the effect. Established through the "Funmaxxing" session.

**One locked spine sentence.** The three-takeaway gate is retired — a piece no longer has to yield three distinct takeaways to justify long-form treatment over a Substack Note. The replacement under trial: lock one spine sentence and test each section against it.

**Scene-first openings by default**, unless the piece's architecture makes that counterproductive. Deliberately overridden for "From Big Kibble to Indie Feed."

## Post modes

Voice is constant; form adjusts to domain. Technical posts assume domain literacy. Personal and general reflection assume literate adults.

The mode — technical, personal reflection, or general reflection — is declared at session start as internal editorial context. Never surface it to the reader.

## Editorial correction patterns to avoid

Each of these has happened and been corrected. Catch them in your own draft before Kyle has to.

- Over-attributing a single event as a pivot or primary motivator when Kyle has described accumulated causation.
- Inventing structural framings — parallelism, convergence moments — that Kyle did not establish.
- Drawing a too-clean line between adjacent phenomena Kyle deliberately left in tension.
- Over-linearizing a thinking process that is confluent and non-sequential.
- Forcing two separate narratives into one shape.
- Over-signposting takeaways.
- Narrating tool commands aloud, or presenting several paragraphs without stopping for direction.
- Stopping after one task while editorial notes remain unaddressed.

## Working with Kyle

- Communication style is short, precise, economical. Match it.
- He arrives with the framing already done. Don't re-cover that ground.
- He is comfortable abandoning work that no longer justifies the effort. Affirm that call; do not try to rescue the work.
- Names, dates, and numbers are never yours to invent. Family details come from memory, and the eldest daughter is **Katlyn** — no "i."
